# Amateur radio

![Amateur radio workspace]({{MEDIA_URL}}/wiki/radio.en.png)

*The image shows macOS; the Windows layout may differ.*

MorseCore helps prepare CW messages and keep a local contact log. It does **not** connect to a transceiver or transmit RF. Listening to generated Morse is local audio playback.

## 1. Add your station

Open **Amateur radio → My stations**. Enter your call sign and run identification. Check the suggested country and licence class against your actual licence, then save. You can keep multiple stations. Prefix recognition does not verify ownership, assignment or validity.

## 2. Prepare a contact

Open **Prepare a contact** and expand the station/frequency section. Select your station, operating country, local licence class and operating setup. If operating away from home, confirm the complete operating call and any required suffix; foreign operating privileges are not assumed.

Listen first. The first message stage checks whether the frequency is occupied (`QRL?`). Then choose a general call, reply, observed signal report, message or closing. Review the generated text before playing or saving its Morse audio. International CW templates use Latin letters for names and locations. Prosigns such as `<AR>` are sent as joined signals.

## 3. Save and find contacts

Expand **Add this contact to the log**. Records default to **practice**; mark an actual contact only after a real radio exchange. Dates use **UTC**. **Contact log** separates practice and actual contacts, with search and editing.

Use **Import / export files** for ADIF or CSV. ADIF import previews supported CW records before confirmation, skips exact duplicates, and accepts ASCII `.adi`/`.adif` files up to 8 MiB and 10,000 records. Keep the original: unsupported ADIF fields are not preserved. CSV retains Unicode notes.

The offline **Contact map** uses entered coordinates or Maidenhead locators. A call prefix does not locate the operator.

## Country and band guide

Selected CW references cover Türkiye, Germany, Russia, the UK, USA, Saudi Arabia and UAE, plus **Other**. Sources were checked on **15 October 2026**. Russia has no automatic frequency/power match. Read the source links and conditions in the guide; a catalog match is not operating permission or a complete band plan.
